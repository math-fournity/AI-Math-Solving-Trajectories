An $n \times n$ array of integers is called *regular* if each row and each column contains at most $k$ distinct integers. Let $R(n, k)$ be the maximum number of cells in an $n \times n$ array such that no integer is located in at least 3 rows and at least 3 columns. Find the maximum value of $n$ such that for any regular $n \times n$ array with $k=6$, there must exist an integer in at least 3 rows and at least 3 columns.
