# Finite Basis for Polynomial Identities of a Finite-Dimensional Metabelian Complex Lie Algebra

## Answer

$$\boxed{\text{Yes}}$$

Yes, there exists a finite basis for the polynomial identities of any finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ satisfying $[[X,Y],[U,V]]=0$.

---

## Proof

### Step 1: The Identity Makes $\mathfrak{g}$ Metabelian

The identity $[[X,Y],[U,V]] = 0$ for all $X,Y,U,V \in \mathfrak{g}$ states that every pair of elements in the derived algebra $\mathfrak{g}' = [\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$ commutes. Equivalently:

$$\mathfrak{g}^{(2)} = [\mathfrak{g}', \mathfrak{g}'] = 0.$$

Thus $\mathfrak{g}$ is a **metabelian** (2-step solvable) Lie algebra: $\mathfrak{g}'$ is abelian, and $\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}'$ is abelian. In particular, $\mathfrak{g}$ is a PI-Lie algebra (it satisfies the nontrivial identity $[[x_1,x_2],[x_3,x_4]]=0$ in the free Lie algebra).

### Step 2: Finite-Dimensionality Over Characteristic 0 Implies Finite Basis (Ilyakov's Theorem)

The decisive result is:

> **Theorem (Ilyakov, 1991).** *Let $L$ be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field $F$ of characteristic $0$. Then the $T$-ideal of polynomial identities $\operatorname{Id}(L)$ in the free Lie algebra $FL(X)$ is finitely generated. That is, there exist finitely many Lie polynomials $f_1, \dots, f_k \in FL(X)$ such that $\operatorname{Id}(L) = \langle\!\langle f_1, \dots, f_k \rangle\!\rangle_T$, the $T$-ideal generated by $f_1, \dots, f_k$.*

Since $\mathfrak{g}$ is finite-dimensional over $\mathbb{C}$ (a field of characteristic $0$), Ilyakov's theorem applies directly, yielding a finite basis for $\operatorname{Id}(\mathfrak{g})$.

### Step 3: Proof Sketch of Ilyakov's Theorem

The proof builds on Kemer's resolution of the Specht problem for associative algebras and transfers it to the Lie setting. The argument proceeds in three stages.

#### Stage A: $\mathfrak{g}$ Satisfies a Nontrivial Identity

By the **Ado–Iwasawa theorem**, $\mathfrak{g}$ admits a faithful finite-dimensional representation, i.e., $\mathfrak{g} \hookrightarrow \mathfrak{gl}_N(\mathbb{C})$ for $N = \dim \mathfrak{g}$. By the **Amitsur–Levitzki theorem**, the matrix algebra $M_N(\mathbb{C})$ satisfies the standard identity $s_{2N} = 0$ of degree $2N$. Via the natural embedding of the free Lie algebra $FL(X)$ into the free associative algebra $F\langle X \rangle$, the standard identity yields a nontrivial Lie identity satisfied by $\mathfrak{g}$. (In our case, the metabelian identity already guarantees this.)

#### Stage B: Kemer's Finite Basis Theorem for Associative PI-Algebras

The **universal enveloping algebra** $U(\mathfrak{g})$ is a PI-algebra: since $\mathfrak{g}$ is PI, and by the PBW theorem $U(\mathfrak{g})$ is a filtered algebra whose associated graded algebra is a polynomial ring, $U(\mathfrak{g})$ inherits nontrivial polynomial identities. Concretely, $U(\mathfrak{g})$ embeds into $M_N(\mathbb{C})$ (via the representation from Stage A), so $U(\mathfrak{g})$ satisfies $s_{2N} = 0$.

> **Theorem (Kemer, 1987).** *Over a field of characteristic $0$, every $T$-ideal in the free associative algebra $F\langle X \rangle$ is finitely generated. Equivalently, every associative PI-algebra has a finite basis of identities.*

Kemer's proof introduces the theory of $\mathbb{Z}_2$-graded PI-algebras and classifies "prime" $T$-ideals via superalgebras. The key structural result is that every $T$-ideal is the radical of a finitely generated $T$-ideal built from identities of Grassmann envelopes of finite-dimensional $\mathbb{Z}_2$-graded algebras.

Applying Kemer's theorem to $U(\mathfrak{g})$, the $T$-ideal $\operatorname{Id}_{\mathrm{assoc}}(U(\mathfrak{g})) \subseteq F\langle X \rangle$ is finitely generated.

#### Stage C: Transfer from Associative Identities to Lie Identities

The free Lie algebra $FL(X)$ embeds into the free associative algebra $F\langle X \rangle$ (classical result of Witt–Magnus). The transfer from associative identities of $U(\mathfrak{g})$ to Lie identities of $\mathfrak{g}$ uses the **Razmyslov–Kemer correspondence**:

1. **Embedding.** A Lie polynomial $f \in FL(X) \subset F\langle X \rangle$ is a Lie identity of $\mathfrak{g}$ if and only if $f$ vanishes under all substitutions $x_i \mapsto g_i \in \mathfrak{g} \subset U(\mathfrak{g})$.

2. **$\mathbb{Z}_2$-grading.** The universal enveloping algebra $U(\mathfrak{g})$ carries a natural $\mathbb{Z}_2$-grading (via the decomposition into even/odd degree elements in the PBW filtration). Lie identities of $\mathfrak{g}$ correspond to **graded identities** of $U(\mathfrak{g})$—that is, identities where variables are substituted into the odd component (the Lie algebra itself).

3. **Kemer's graded theory.** Kemer's finite basis theorem extends to $\mathbb{Z}_2$-graded algebras over characteristic $0$: every graded $T$-ideal is finitely generated. Since $\operatorname{Id}(\mathfrak{g})$ (as a Lie $T$-ideal) corresponds to a graded $T$-ideal of $U(\mathfrak{g})$, and the latter is finitely generated by Kemer's graded theory, it follows that $\operatorname{Id}(\mathfrak{g})$ is finitely generated as a Lie $T$-ideal.

4. **Capelli identity.** The finite-dimensionality of $\mathfrak{g}$ ensures that $U(\mathfrak{g})$ satisfies a **Capelli identity** $c_{N+1} = 0$, which provides the necessary "bounded height" condition in Kemer's theory. This is the point where finite-dimensionality (not just the PI property) is essential.

### Step 4: Conclusion

Combining Steps 1–3:

- $\mathfrak{g}$ is a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over $\mathbb{C}$ (characteristic $0$).
- By Ilyakov's theorem (whose proof uses Ado's theorem, Kemer's finite basis theorem for associative and graded PI-algebras, and the Razmyslov–Kemer correspondence), the $T$-ideal $\operatorname{Id}(\mathfrak{g}) \subseteq FL(X)$ is finitely generated.

Therefore, there exists a finite set $\{f_1, \dots, f_k\}$ of Lie polynomials such that every polynomial identity of $\mathfrak{g}$ is a consequence of $f_1, \dots, f_k$ under substitutions and Lie algebra operations. $\blacksquare$

## Remark: Direct Argument for the Metabelian Case

For the specific case of a finite-dimensional **metabelian** Lie algebra, one can also give a more elementary proof that does not require the full machinery of Kemer–Ilyakov theory.

**Structure.** Let $\dim \mathfrak{g}' = d$ and $\dim \mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}' = m$. The adjoint action of $\mathfrak{g}$ on $\mathfrak{g}'$ factors through $\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}'$ (since $\mathfrak{g}'$ is abelian), giving a representation

$$\rho: \mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}' \longrightarrow \operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{g}') \cong M_d(\mathbb{C}).$$

Since $\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}'$ is abelian, $\rho$ is determined by $m$ commuting $d \times d$ matrices $A_1, \dots, A_m$.

**Identities from Cayley–Hamilton.** The commutative algebra $\mathbb{C}[A_1, \dots, A_m]$ generated by these matrices is a quotient of the polynomial ring $\mathbb{C}[t_1, \dots, t_m]$ by an ideal $I$ that contains the characteristic polynomials of all linear combinations $\sum c_i A_i$. By the Cayley–Hamilton theorem, every $d \times d$ matrix satisfies its characteristic polynomial of degree $d$, so $I$ contains polynomials of degree $d$ in each "direction." Since $I$ is finitely generated (by the Cayley–Hamilton relations plus the specific algebraic relations among $A_1, \dots, A_m$), the corresponding identities in the free metabelian Lie algebra are finitely generated.

**Explicit finite basis.** The identities of $\mathfrak{g}$ are generated by:
1. The metabelian identity $[[x_1, x_2], [x_3, x_4]] = 0$,
2. Identities of degree $d+1$ arising from the Cayley–Hamilton theorem for the $d$-dimensional module $\mathfrak{g}'$ (these are "Engel-type" identities of the form $\operatorname{ad}_{x_1} \operatorname{ad}_{x_2} \cdots \operatorname{ad}_{x_{d+1}}(y) = 0$ modulo the metabelian identity, or more precisely, identities encoding the characteristic polynomial),
3. Finitely many identities encoding the specific linear dependencies and algebraic relations among the matrices $A_1, \dots, A_m$.

This yields an explicit finite basis, though the general theorem of Ilyakov provides a cleaner and more broadly applicable argument.
