Two people, A and B, play a number guessing game. A chooses a positive integer $a$ such that $1 \leqslant a \leqslant 2006$. B's goal is to determine $a$ by asking questions. Each time B tells A a positive integer $b$, A tells B whether $a+b$ is a prime number. Let $N$ be the minimum number of questions $b_1, b_2, \dots, b_N$ such that for any two distinct choices $a_1, a_2 \in \{1, 2, \dots, 2006\}$, the sequences of answers $(f(a_1+b_1), \dots, f(a_1+b_N))$ and $(f(a_2+b_1), \dots, f(a_2+b_N))$ are different, where $f(n)=1$ if $n$ is prime and $f(n)=0$ otherwise. Find the smallest integer $M$ such that the statement "B can always guess the number $a$ with $M$ questions" is guaranteed by the proof that no two distinct integers $a_1, a_2 \in \{1, \dots, 2006\}$ yield the same primality results for $b \in \{1, 2, \dots, M\}$.
