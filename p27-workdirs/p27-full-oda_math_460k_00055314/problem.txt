We assume that \( f(x) \) IS measurable (\( L \)), periodic, \( f(x + 1) = f(x) \) and \( f(x) = \pm 1 \). Do we have, almost everywhere, \(\limsup_{n \to \infty} f(nx) = +1, \quad \liminf_{n \to \infty} f(nx) = -1?\) More generally: If \( f_n(x) \) are measurable, uniformly bounded, and \( f_n(x + 1/n) \equiv f_n(x) \), do we have then \(\limsup_{n \to \infty} f_n(x) = \text{constant almost everywhere?}\) \(\liminf_{n \to \infty} f_n(x) = \text{constant almost everywhere?}\)
