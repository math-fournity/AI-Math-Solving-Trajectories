Consider a probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)$ and the real line with the Borel sigma-algebra $(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}),\lambda)$. Let $(\Omega\times\mathbb{R},\mathcal{F}\otimes \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}),P\otimes \lambda)$ be the product measurable space. Given a set $A$ that is measurable in $\mathcal{F}\otimes \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$ with $P\otimes\lambda (A)>0$, determine if there exists an $x\in\mathbb{R}$ and a set $B\in\mathcal{F}$ with $P(B)>0$ such that $B\times\{x\}\subset A$. Provide a justification for your answer.
