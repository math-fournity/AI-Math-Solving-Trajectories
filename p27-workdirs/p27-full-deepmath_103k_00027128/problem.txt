Consider two continuous functions $g_1, g_2: \overline{\mathbb{D}} \to [0,\infty)$, where $\mathbb{D} := \{z\in\mathbb{C}\;|\;|z| < 1\}$. Define $g := g_1 - g_2$. Assume $g_2(0) = 0$, $g_2(z) > 0$ for all $z\in\mathbb{S}^1$, $g_1(z) > 0$ for all $z\in \overline{\mathbb{D}}$, and the connected components of $Z_2 := \{z\in\mathbb{C}\;|\;g_2(z) = 0\}$ are simply connected. Determine if there exists a simple and closed curve $\gamma \subset \overline{\mathbb{D}}$, homeomorphic to $\mathbb{S}^1$, such that $0\in \text{int }\gamma$, $g_2(z) > 0$ for all $z\in \gamma$, and $g(z) > 0$ for all $z\in \gamma\cup\text{int }\gamma$. Provide a justification for your answer.
