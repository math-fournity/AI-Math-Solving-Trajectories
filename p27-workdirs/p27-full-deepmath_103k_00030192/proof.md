# Proof

**Answer: No.** We construct an explicit flat morphism $\psi: X \to \mathbb{A}^1_{\mathbb{C}}$ and two nonzero points $c_1, c_2 \in \mathbb{A}^1_{\mathbb{C}}$ such that $\psi^{-1}(c_1)$ is a complete intersection but $\psi^{-1}(c_2)$ is not.

## Counterexample

Define
$$X = \operatorname{Spec}\!\Big(\mathbb{C}[x,y,t]\,\big/\,\big(x^2 - t(t-1)\,y,\; xy,\; y^2\big)\Big),$$
and let $\psi: X \to \mathbb{A}^1_{\mathbb{C}} = \operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[t])$ be the morphism induced by the natural inclusion $\mathbb{C}[t] \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}[x,y,t]/I$. Set $c_1 = 2$ and $c_2 = 1$; both are nonzero.

## Step 1: Flatness of $\psi$

Let $I = (x^2 - t(t-1)y,\; xy,\; y^2) \subset \mathbb{C}[x,y,t]$. Use the lexicographic term order with $x > y > t$. The leading terms of the three generators are $x^2$, $xy$, and $y^2$ respectively, so the initial ideal is
$$\operatorname{in}(I) = (x^2,\; xy,\; y^2).$$
The standard monomials (monomials not in $\operatorname{in}(I)$) are
$$\{t^a,\; t^a x,\; t^a y : a \geq 0\}.$$
By the standard theory of Gröbner bases, the coordinate ring $A = \mathbb{C}[x,y,t]/I$ is a free $\mathbb{C}[t]$-module with basis $\{1, x, y\}$. Being free (hence torsion-free) over the PID $\mathbb{C}[t]$, the module $A$ is flat over $\mathbb{C}[t]$. Therefore $\psi$ is a flat morphism. $\checkmark$

(Equivalently, every fiber has length $3$: the relation $x^3 = x \cdot x^2 = x \cdot t(t-1)y = t(t-1) \cdot xy = 0$ shows that the only surviving monomials in each fiber are $1, x, y$, giving constant length $3$.)

## Step 2: The fiber at $t = 2$ is a complete intersection

At $t = 2$, the fiber is $\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[x,y]/J_2)$ where
$$J_2 = (x^2 - 2y,\; xy,\; y^2) \subset \mathbb{C}[x,y].$$
Since $x^2 - 2y = 0$ gives $y = x^2/2$, we can substitute:
- $xy = x \cdot x^2/2 = x^3/2 = 0 \implies x^3 = 0$,
- $y^2 = x^4/4 = 0$, which follows from $x^3 = 0$.

Thus
$$J_2 = \big(y - \tfrac{x^2}{2},\; x^3\big).$$
This is a regular sequence in $\mathbb{C}[x,y]$: the element $y - x^2/2$ is a nonzero divisor in $\mathbb{C}[x,y]$ (it is a linear polynomial in $y$), and $x^3$ is a nonzero divisor in the quotient $\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(y - x^2/2) \cong \mathbb{C}[x]$. The ideal is generated by $2$ elements in a ring of dimension $2$, with the quotient having dimension $0$, so the fiber is a complete intersection. $\checkmark$

## Step 3: The fiber at $t = 1$ is NOT a complete intersection

At $t = 1$, we have $t(t-1) = 0$, so the fiber is $\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[x,y]/J_1)$ where
$$J_1 = (x^2,\; xy,\; y^2) = \mathfrak{m}^2 \subset \mathbb{C}[x,y],$$
with $\mathfrak{m} = (x, y)$ the maximal ideal at the origin. This is a $0$-dimensional scheme of length $3$ supported at a single point.

The quotient $\mathbb{C}[x,y]/\mathfrak{m}^2$ has Krull dimension $0$, so the codimension of $V(\mathfrak{m}^2)$ in $\mathbb{A}^2$ is $2$. For this to be a complete intersection, the ideal $\mathfrak{m}^2$ would need to be generated by exactly $2$ elements (a regular sequence of length equal to the codimension).

However, the minimal number of generators of $\mathfrak{m}^2$ is
$$\mu(\mathfrak{m}^2) = \dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{m}^2/\mathfrak{m}\cdot\mathfrak{m}^2) = \dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{m}^2/\mathfrak{m}^3) = 3,$$
since $\mathfrak{m}^2/\mathfrak{m}^3$ has basis $\{x^2, xy, y^2\}$ over $\mathbb{C}$. Since $3 > 2 = \operatorname{codim}(V(\mathfrak{m}^2))$, the ideal $\mathfrak{m}^2$ cannot be generated by a regular sequence of length $2$. Therefore the fiber at $t = 1$ is **not** a complete intersection. $\checkmark$

## Conclusion

We have constructed a flat morphism $\psi: X \to \mathbb{A}^1_{\mathbb{C}}$ such that:
- $\psi^{-1}(2)$ is a complete intersection (with $2 \neq 0$),
- $\psi^{-1}(1)$ is not a complete intersection (with $1 \neq 0$).

Therefore, it does **not** follow that all nonzero fibers of a flat morphism to $\mathbb{A}^1_{\mathbb{C}}$ are complete intersections merely because one nonzero fiber is.

$$\boxed{\text{No}}$$
