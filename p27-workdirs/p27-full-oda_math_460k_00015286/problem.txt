Consider a group \( G \) with cardinality strictly greater than that of the continuum. Construct a metric space \( M \) such that \( G \) is its full group of isometries. If such a construction is possible, determine the minimum number of distinct distances that must exist in \( M \) to ensure that \( G \) is indeed the full group of isometries of \( M \). If the construction is not possible, provide the cardinality of the smallest set \( S \) such that no metric space can be constructed with \( S \) as its full group of isometries.
