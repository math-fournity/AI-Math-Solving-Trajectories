Let \(ABC\) be an obtuse triangle, and let \(H\) denote its orthocenter. Let \(\omega_A\) be the circle with center \(A\) and radius \(AH\), and define \(\omega_B\) and \(\omega_C\) similarly. For all points \(X\) in the plane of triangle \(ABC\), define circle \(\Omega(X)\) as the circumcircle of the triangle formed by the polars of point \(X\) with respect to circles \(\omega_A\), \(\omega_B\), and \(\omega_C\). Find the locus of points \(X\) for which point \(X\) lies on circle \(\Omega(X)\), with a possible exception of finitely many points.
