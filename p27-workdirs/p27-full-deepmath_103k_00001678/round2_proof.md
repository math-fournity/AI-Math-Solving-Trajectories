# Proof

**Claim:** The answer is **No**. There exists a field $k$ and a $k$-algebra $R$ such that $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra for every prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of $R$, yet $R$ is not a finitely generated $k$-algebra.

## Counterexample

Let $k$ be any field. Define

$$R = \{f: \mathbb{N} \to k : f \text{ is eventually constant}\},$$

i.e., $R$ is the ring of all sequences $(a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots)$ with values in $k$ that are eventually constant, equipped with pointwise addition and multiplication. The map $c \mapsto (c, c, c, \ldots)$ embeds $k$ into $R$ as the subring of constant sequences, making $R$ a commutative $k$-algebra.

For each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, let $e_n \in R$ denote the sequence that is $1$ at position $n$ and $0$ elsewhere. Then:

- $e_n^2 = e_n$ (idempotent),
- $e_n \cdot e_m = 0$ for $n \neq m$ (orthogonal),
- $1 - e_n \in R$ is the sequence that is $0$ at position $n$ and $1$ elsewhere (eventually constant $1$).

Every element $f \in R$ can be written uniquely as $f = c \cdot 1 + \sum_{n \in S} a_n e_n$ where $c \in k$, $S \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ is finite, and $a_n \in k$.

## Step 1: The prime ideals of $R$

Define:
- $\mathfrak{m}_n = \{f \in R : f(n) = 0\} = (1 - e_n)R$ for each $n \in \mathbb{N}$,
- $\mathfrak{m}_\infty = \{f \in R : f \text{ is eventually } 0\} = \bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty} k \cdot e_n$.

**These are maximal ideals with residue field $k$:**

- $R/\mathfrak{m}_n \cong k$ via the evaluation map $f \mapsto f(n)$.
- $R/\mathfrak{m}_\infty \cong k$ via the map $f \mapsto c$ where $c$ is the eventual value of $f$.

**These are the only prime ideals.** Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be any prime ideal of $R$. For each $n$, since $e_n(1 - e_n) = 0 \in \mathfrak{p}$, primality gives $e_n \in \mathfrak{p}$ or $1 - e_n \in \mathfrak{p}$.

- **Case 1:** $1 - e_n \in \mathfrak{p}$ for some $n$. Then $\mathfrak{m}_n = (1-e_n)R \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$. Since $\mathfrak{m}_n$ is maximal, $\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak{m}_n$.

- **Case 2:** $e_n \in \mathfrak{p}$ for all $n$. Then $\mathfrak{m}_\infty = \sum_n e_n R \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$. Since $\mathfrak{m}_\infty$ is maximal, $\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak{m}_\infty$.

Thus $\operatorname{Spec}(R) = \{\mathfrak{m}_n : n \in \mathbb{N}\} \cup \{\mathfrak{m}_\infty\}$, and every prime is maximal.

## Step 2: Every localization $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra

**Localization at $\mathfrak{m}_n$:** In $R_{\mathfrak{m}_n}$, the element $e_n$ is a unit (since $e_n \notin \mathfrak{m}_n$, as $e_n(n) = 1 \neq 0$). From $e_n(1 - e_n) = 0$ with $e_n$ a unit, we get $1 - e_n = 0$ in $R_{\mathfrak{m}_n}$, i.e., $e_n = 1$. For $m \neq n$, $e_m \cdot e_n = 0$ with $e_n = 1$ gives $e_m = 0$. Hence every generator $e_m$ is either $0$ or $1$, and

$$R_{\mathfrak{m}_n} \cong k,$$

which is a finitely generated $k$-algebra (generated by the empty set, or equivalently by $0$ and $1$).

**Localization at $\mathfrak{m}_\infty$:** In $R_{\mathfrak{m}_\infty}$, the element $1 - e_n$ is a unit for every $n$ (since $1 - e_n \notin \mathfrak{m}_\infty$, as $1 - e_n$ is eventually $1 \neq 0$). From $e_n(1 - e_n) = 0$ with $1 - e_n$ a unit, we get $e_n = 0$ for all $n$. Hence

$$R_{\mathfrak{m}_\infty} \cong k,$$

again a finitely generated $k$-algebra.

## Step 3: $R$ is not a finitely generated $k$-algebra

Suppose for contradiction that $R = k[a_1, \ldots, a_m]$ for some $a_1, \ldots, a_m \in R$. Each $a_i$ is eventually constant, so there exists $N \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $a_i(n) = a_i(N)$ for all $n \geq N$ and all $i = 1, \ldots, m$.

Any element of $k[a_1, \ldots, a_m]$ is of the form $p(a_1, \ldots, a_m)$ for some polynomial $p \in k[x_1, \ldots, x_m]$. For $n \geq N$:

$$p(a_1, \ldots, a_m)(n) = p(a_1(n), \ldots, a_m(n)) = p(a_1(N), \ldots, a_m(N)),$$

which is constant (independent of $n$). Therefore every element of $k[a_1, \ldots, a_m]$ is constant for $n \geq N$.

However, $e_{N+1} \in R$ satisfies $e_{N+1}(N+1) = 1$ and $e_{N+1}(N+2) = 0$, so $e_{N+1}$ is **not** constant for $n \geq N$. This contradicts $R = k[a_1, \ldots, a_m]$. $\square$

## Conclusion

$$\boxed{No}$$

The $k$-algebra $R = \{f: \mathbb{N} \to k : f \text{ is eventually constant}\}$ satisfies the hypothesis that $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra for every prime $\mathfrak{p}$ (in fact $R_{\mathfrak{p}} \cong k$ for every prime), but $R$ itself is not finitely generated as a $k$-algebra. This counterexample works for every field $k$.
