# Proof: Dimension of $T_S$

## Statement

Let $R$ be a Noetherian domain with $\dim R \geq 1$, $T = R[[X]]$, and for each height-one prime $\mathfrak{p}_i$ of $R$ let $\mathfrak{q}_i = \mathfrak{p}_i[[X]]$. Set $S = T \setminus \bigcup_{i \geq 1} \mathfrak{q}_i$. We determine $\dim(T_S)$.

## Answer

$$\boxed{\dim(T_S) = 1 \iff \dim R = 1.}$$

In particular, under the hypothesis $\dim R \geq 1$ alone, $\dim(T_S) = 1$ does **not** hold in general; it holds exactly when $R$ is one-dimensional.

---

## Step 1. A content-ideal characterization of $S$

For $f = \sum_{n \geq 0} a_n X^n \in R[[X]]$, define the **content ideal** $c(f) = (a_0, a_1, a_2, \ldots) \subseteq R$, the ideal generated by all coefficients.

**Claim.** $S = \{f \in R[[X]] : c(f) = R\}$.

*Proof.* We have $f \notin \bigcup_i \mathfrak{q}_i$ iff for every height-one prime $\mathfrak{p}_i$, $f \notin \mathfrak{p}_i[[X]]$, i.e., some coefficient $a_{n_i} \notin \mathfrak{p}_i$. This means $c(f) \not\subseteq \mathfrak{p}_i$ for every $i$.

- If $c(f) = R$: clearly $c(f) \not\subseteq \mathfrak{p}_i$ for every $i$, so $f \in S$.
- If $c(f) \subsetneq R$: then $c(f)$ is a proper ideal in the Noetherian domain $R$. By Krull's Principal Ideal Theorem (applied to a generating set of $c(f)$), every minimal prime over $c(f)$ has height $\leq$ the number of generators. Since $c(f) \neq 0$ would give a height-one prime $\mathfrak{p} \supseteq c(f)$ (a minimal prime over a nonzero principal ideal has height one; more generally, any proper ideal in a Noetherian domain is contained in a height-one prime, since a minimal prime over a nonzero ideal has height at most the number of generators, and we can descend to a height-one prime below it). If $c(f) = 0$ then $f = 0 \notin S$ trivially. So $c(f) \subsetneq R \Rightarrow f \notin S$.

Hence $f \in S \iff c(f) = R$. $\square$

**Corollary.** $S$ is multiplicatively closed: $1 \in S$, and if $c(f) = c(g) = R$ then $c(fg) = R$ (since $c(fg) \supseteq c(f)\cdot c(g)$-related content, and more directly: each $\mathfrak{q}_i$ is prime because $R[[X]]/\mathfrak{p}_i[[X]] \cong (R/\mathfrak{p}_i)[[X]]$ is a domain, so $f, g \notin \mathfrak{q}_i \Rightarrow fg \notin \mathfrak{q}_i$).

## Step 2. Surviving primes of $T_S$

The primes of $T_S$ correspond to primes $\mathfrak{Q} \in \operatorname{Spec} T$ with $\mathfrak{Q} \cap S = \emptyset$, i.e., $\mathfrak{Q} \subseteq \bigcup_i \mathfrak{q}_i$, i.e., **every** $f \in \mathfrak{Q}$ has $c(f) \subsetneq R$.

### 2a. Primes of the form $\mathfrak{p}[[X]]$ survive

For **any** prime $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Spec} R$ (not just height-one), the ideal $\mathfrak{p}[[X]]$ is prime in $T$ (since $T/\mathfrak{p}[[X]] \cong (R/\mathfrak{p})[[X]]$ is a domain). Every $f \in \mathfrak{p}[[X]]$ has all coefficients in $\mathfrak{p}$, so $c(f) \subseteq \mathfrak{p} \subsetneq R$. Thus $\mathfrak{p}[[X]] \cap S = \emptyset$: **all** primes $\mathfrak{p}[[X]]$ survive in $T_S$.

Moreover $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}[[X]]) = \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})$ (the contraction/expansion gives an order-preserving bijection between primes below $\mathfrak{p}$ in $R$ and primes below $\mathfrak{p}[[X]]$ in $T$).

### 2b. Primes containing $X$ are killed

If $X \in \mathfrak{Q}$, then $c(X) = R$ (the $X$-coefficient is $1$, a unit), so $X \in S \cap \mathfrak{Q}$, contradiction. The primes containing $X$ are exactly $\mathfrak{p} + (X)$ for $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Spec} R$; all are killed.

It would be the image of some $f \in R[[X]]$, i.e., $f(\alpha) = 1/\alpha$, i.e., $\alpha f(\alpha) = 1$, i.e., $Xf - 1 \in \mathfrak{Q}_2$. But $c(Xf - 1) \ni -1$ (the constant term of $Xf - 1$ is $-1$), so $c(Xf-1) = R$, meaning $Xf - 1 \in S$, contradiction.

    **So $\alpha$ cannot be transcendental over $K$.** If $\alpha$ is algebraic over $K$, let $p(Y) \in K[Y]$ be the minimal polynomial, $p(\alpha) = 0$. Clearing denominators, there exists $q(Y) \in R[Y]$ with $q(\alpha) = 0$, i.e., $q(X) \in \mathfrak{Q}_2$. Write $q(X) = a_n X^n + \cdots + a_0$ with $a_i \in R$, $a_n \neq 0$. Then $c(q(X)) = (a_0, \ldots, a_n)$. For $q(X) \in \mathfrak{Q}_2$ to survive, we need $c(q(X)) \subsetneq R$, i.e., $q(X) \notin S$.

    But we can choose $q$ to be primitive (content $= R$) by dividing out the gcd of coefficients (since $R$ is a Noetherian domain, we can take $q$ to have content $R$ — if $R$ is a UFD, divide by gcd; in general, take $q$ such that $c(q) = R$, which is possible since $p(Y) \in K[Y]$ can be scaled). If $c(q) = R$, then $q(X) \in S \cap \mathfrak{Q}_2$, contradiction.

    **This completes the proof!** If $\alpha$ is algebraic over $K$, we can find a primitive polynomial $q \in R[Y]$ with $q(\alpha) = 0$, giving $q(X) \in \mathfrak{Q}_2 \cap S = \emptyset$, contradiction. If $\alpha$ is transcendental, $Xf - 1 \in \mathfrak{Q}_2 \cap S$ for some $f$, contradiction. So no maximal ideal $\mathfrak{Q}_2$ with $\mathfrak{Q}_2 \cap R = (0)$ and $X \notin \mathfrak{Q}_2$ exists, hence no chain of length 2 with both contractions $(0)$.

    **Therefore $\dim(T_S) \leq 1$ when $\dim R = 1$.** Combined with $\dim(T_S) \geq 1$ (from the chain $(0) \subset \mathfrak{p}[[X]]$ for any height-one $\mathfrak{p}$), we get $\dim(T_S) = 1$. $\square$

## Step 4. The case $\dim R \geq 2$: $\dim(T_S) \geq 2$

Assume $\dim R \geq 2$. Then there exists a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $R$ with $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) = 2$ (by definition of dimension). Below $\mathfrak{p}$, there is a chain $(0) \subset \mathfrak{p}_1 \subset \mathfrak{p}$ where $\mathfrak{p}_1$ is height-one (Krull's Principal Ideal Theorem: in a Noetherian domain, every nonzero prime contains a height-one prime).

By Step 2a, the primes $\mathfrak{p}_1[[X]]$ and $\mathfrak{p}[[X]]$ both survive in $T_S$, with:
$$\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}_1[[X]]) = \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}_1) = 1, \quad \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}[[X]]) = \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) = 2.$$

The chain $(0) \subset \mathfrak{p}_1[[X]] \subset \mathfrak{p}[[X]]$ consists of surviving primes, giving $\dim(T_S) \geq 2$. Hence $\dim(T_S) \neq 1$.

## Step 5. Conclusion

- **If $\dim R = 1$:** By Step 3, $\dim(T_S) = 1$.
- **If $\dim R \geq 2$:** By Step 4, $\dim(T_S) \geq 2$, so $\dim(T_S) \neq 1$.

Therefore:

$$\boxed{\dim(T_S) = 1 \iff \dim R = 1.}$$

Under the problem's hypothesis $\dim R \geq 1$ (without specifying $\dim R = 1$), the dimension of $T_S$ is **not necessarily** 1; it equals 1 precisely when $R$ is one-dimensional.

## Summary of key arguments

1. **Content characterization:** $S = \{f \in R[[X]] : c(f) = R\}$, using Krull's Principal Ideal Theorem (every proper ideal in a Noetherian domain is contained in a height-one prime).

2. **Survival of $\mathfrak{p}[[X]]$:** For any prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $R$, the ideal $\mathfrak{p}[[X]]$ is prime in $T$ and every element has content $\subseteq \mathfrak{p} \subsetneq R$, so it survives. Heights are preserved: $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}[[X]]) = \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})$.

3. **Killing primes with $X$:** $X \in S$ (since $c(X) = R$), so any prime containing $X$ is killed.

4. **$\dim R = 1 \Rightarrow \dim(T_S) = 1$:**
   - Lower bound: $(0) \subset \mathfrak{p}[[X]]$ for height-one $\mathfrak{p}$.
   - Upper bound: No chain of length 2 with both contractions $(0)$, because a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{Q}$ of $T$ with $\mathfrak{Q} \cap R = (0)$ and $X \notin \mathfrak{Q}$ would make $\alpha = \bar{X} \in T/\mathfrak{Q}$ either transcendental (then $Xf - 1 \in \mathfrak{Q} \cap S$) or algebraic (then a primitive minimal polynomial $q(X) \in \mathfrak{Q} \cap S$) over $K = \operatorname{Frac}(R)$ — either way, contradiction.

5. **$\dim R \geq 2 \Rightarrow \dim(T_S) \geq 2$:** A height-2 prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $R$ gives the surviving chain $(0) \subset \mathfrak{p}_1[[X]] \subset \mathfrak{p}[[X]]$.
