# Theorem

Let $X$ be a Noetherian scheme that is proper over a field $k$, and let $\mathcal{L}$ be an ample line bundle on $X$. Then there exists an integer $n \geq 1$ such that the canonical map

$$\varphi_n : X \longrightarrow \mathbb{P}\bigl(\Gamma(X, \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n})^\vee\bigr)$$

is a closed immersion.

> **Remark on hypotheses.** For $\mathbb{P}(\Gamma(X, \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}))$ to be a finite-dimensional projective space (so that the canonical map is a morphism into projective space in the usual sense) and for the image of a morphism from $X$ to be *closed* in that projective space, one needs $X$ to be proper over $k$. These are the standard hypotheses under which the statement is posed (cf. Hartshorne, Theorem II.7.6); we adopt them throughout.

## Proof

### Amplitude — definition

Recall that a line bundle $\mathcal{L}$ on a Noetherian scheme $X$ is **ample** if, for every coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$, there exists an integer $n_0(\mathcal{F})$ such that $\mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}$ is generated by its global sections for all $n \geq n_0(\mathcal{F})$.

### Closed-immersion criterion

Since $X$ is proper over $k$, a morphism $\varphi_n : X \to \mathbb{P}^N_k$ is a closed immersion if and only if the following three conditions hold (this is the standard criterion; see EGA II, 4.4, or Hartshorne, II.7):

1. **(Generation)** $\mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}$ is generated by its global sections.
2. **(Separation of points)** For every pair of distinct closed points $x \neq y$ in $X$, the evaluation map
$$\Gamma(X, \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}) \longrightarrow \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}|_x \oplus \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}|_y$$
is surjective.
3. **(Separation of tangent vectors)** For every closed point $x \in X$, writing $\mathcal{I}_x$ for the ideal sheaf of the reduced closed point $\{x\}$, the map
$$\Gamma(X, \mathcal{I}_x \otimes \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}) \longrightarrow (\mathcal{I}_x / \mathfrak{m}_x \mathcal{I}_x) \otimes \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}|_x \;\cong\; (\mathfrak{m}_x / \mathfrak{m}_x^2) \otimes_k \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}|_x$$
is surjective.

Indeed, conditions (1)–(3) give that $\varphi_n$ is an immersion (a locally closed immersion), and properness of $X$ over $k$ then forces its image to be closed, hence $\varphi_n$ is a closed immersion.

We now verify (1)–(3) simultaneously for all $n$ large enough.

---

### Step 1 — Global generation (condition 1)

Apply the definition of ampleness to $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{O}_X$. There exists $n_1 \geq 1$ such that for all $n \geq n_1$, the sheaf $\mathcal{L}^{\otimes n} = \mathcal{O}_X \otimes \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}$ is generated by its global sections.

---

### Step 2 — Setup on $X \times X$ (uniform bound for conditions 2 and 3)

Consider $X \times_k X$, which is Noetherian (since $X$ is) and proper over $k$. Let $p_1, p_2 : X \times X \to X$ be the two projections, and set

$$\mathcal{M} \;=\; p_1^* \mathcal{L} \otimes p_2^* \mathcal{L} \;=\; \mathcal{L} \boxtimes \mathcal{L}.$$

**Claim.** $\mathcal{M}$ is ample on $X \times X$.

*Proof of claim.* Since $\mathcal{L}$ is ample on $X$, the line bundle $p_1^*\mathcal{L}$ is ample on the fibers of $p_2$ and trivially so on the fibers of $p_1$, and likewise for $p_2^*\mathcal{L}$. The tensor product of two line bundles that are each ample on one factor is ample on the product (one verifies this directly from the definition: for any coherent $\mathcal{G}$ on $X\times X$, the sheaf $\mathcal{G}\otimes \mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}$ is generated by global sections for $n \gg 0$, using the Künneth formula and the ampleness of $\mathcal{L}$ on each factor). $\square$

Let $\Delta \hookrightarrow X \times X$ be the diagonal, and let $\mathcal{I}_\Delta$ be its ideal sheaf. Since $X \times X$ is Noetherian and $\Delta$ is a closed immersion, $\mathcal{I}_\Delta$ is coherent. By ampleness of $\mathcal{M}$, there exists $n_2 \geq 1$ such that for all $n \geq n_2$,

$$\mathcal{I}_\Delta \otimes \mathcal{M}^{\otimes n} \quad \text{is generated by its global sections.} \tag{$\star$}$$

This single integer $n_2$ will handle both conditions (2) and (3).

---

### Step 3 — Separation of points (condition 2)

Let $x \neq y$ be two distinct closed points of $X$. Then $(x, y) \in X \times X$ does **not** lie on the diagonal $\Delta$, so the stalk $(\mathcal{I}_\Delta)_{(x,y)}$ equals $\mathcal{O}_{X\times X, (x,y)}$. Evaluating the global generation $(\star)$ at $(x,y)$ gives a surjection

$$\Gamma(X\times X,\; \mathcal{I}_\Delta \otimes \mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}) \;\twoheadrightarrow\; \mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}\big|_{(x,y)} \;=\; \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}|_x \otimes_k \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}|_y. \tag{3.1}$$

By the Künneth formula (valid since $X$ is proper over $k$),

$$\Gamma(X\times X,\; \mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}) \;\cong\; \Gamma(X, \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}) \otimes_k \Gamma(X, \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}).$$

A global section of $\mathcal{I}_\Delta \otimes \mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}$ is therefore represented by a finite sum $\sigma = \sum_i s_i \otimes t_i$ with $s_i, t_i \in \Gamma(X, \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n})$, satisfying the vanishing condition $\sum_i s_i(z)\, t_i(z) = 0$ for every $z \in X$ (vanishing along the diagonal). The surjection (3.1) says: there exists such a $\sigma$ with $\sum_i s_i(x)\, t_i(y) \neq 0$.

Fix a trivialization $\mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}|_y \cong k$ and define

$$s \;=\; \sum_i t_i(y)\cdot s_i \;\in\; \Gamma(X, \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}).$$

Then:

- $s(y) = \sum_i t_i(y)\, s_i(y) = \sigma|_{(y,y)} = 0$, since $(y,y)\in\Delta$ and $\sigma$ vanishes on $\Delta$.
- $s(x) = \sum_i t_i(y)\, s_i(x) = \sigma|_{(x,y)} \neq 0$ by the choice of $\sigma$.

Hence $s$ is a global section of $\mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}$ that vanishes at $y$ but not at $x$, proving $\varphi_n(x) \neq \varphi_n(y)$. Since $x, y$ were arbitrary distinct closed points, condition (2) holds for all $n \geq n_2$.

---

### Step 4 — Separation of tangent vectors (condition 3)

Fix a closed point $x \in X$ and consider the diagonal point $(x, x) \in X \times X$. Evaluating $(\star)$ at $(x,x)$ gives a surjection

$$\Gamma(X\times X,\; \mathcal{I}_\Delta \otimes \mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}) \;\twoheadrightarrow\; \bigl(\mathcal{I}_\Delta / \mathfrak{m}_{(x,x)}\,\mathcal{I}_\Delta\bigr) \otimes_k \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}|_x \otimes_k \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}|_x. \tag{4.1}$$

**Lemma.** There is a canonical isomorphism

$$\mathcal{I}_\Delta / \mathfrak{m}_{(x,x)}\,\mathcal{I}_\Delta \;\cong\; \mathfrak{m}_x / \mathfrak{m}_x^2,$$

where $\mathfrak{m}_x \subset \mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ and $\mathfrak{m}_{(x,x)} \subset \mathcal{O}_{X\times X,(x,x)}$ are the maximal ideals.

*Proof of Lemma.* Locally at $(x,x)$, the ideal $\mathcal{I}_\Delta$ is generated by elements of the form $f\otimes 1 - 1\otimes f$ for $f \in \mathcal{O}_{X,x}$. The maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_{(x,x)}$ is generated by $\mathfrak{m}_x \otimes 1$ and $1 \otimes \mathfrak{m}_x$. We analyze the quotient:

- For $f \in \mathfrak{m}_x^2$, write $f = \sum_j a_j b_j$ with $a_j, b_j \in \mathfrak{m}_x$. Then
$$f\otimes 1 - 1\otimes f = \sum_j \bigl(a_j \otimes (b_j\otimes 1 - 1\otimes b_j) + (a_j\otimes 1 - 1\otimes a_j)\otimes b_j\bigr) \in \mathfrak{m}_{(x,x)}\,\mathcal{I}_\Delta,$$
so $f\otimes 1 - 1\otimes f$ vanishes in the quotient.

- For $f \in \mathcal{O}_{X,x}^\times$ (a unit), $f\otimes 1 - 1\otimes f = (f - f(x))\otimes 1 - 1\otimes(f - f(x))$ with $f - f(x) \in \mathfrak{m}_x$, reducing to the case $f \in \mathfrak{m}_x$.

Thus the quotient $\mathcal{I}_\Delta / \mathfrak{m}_{(x,x)}\,\mathcal{I}_\Delta$ is generated by $\{f\otimes 1 - 1\otimes f : f \in \mathfrak{m}_x\}$ modulo $\mathfrak{m}_x^2$, which is exactly $\mathfrak{m}_x / \mathfrak{m}_x^2$. $\square$

Combining the Lemma with (4.1) and fixing a trivialization $\mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}|_x \cong k$, the surjection (4.1) becomes

$$\Gamma(X\times X,\; \mathcal{I}_\Delta \otimes \mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}) \;\twoheadrightarrow\; \mathfrak{m}_x / \mathfrak{m}_x^2 \otimes_k \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}|_x \otimes_k \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}|_x. \tag{4.2}$$

**Extracting sections on $X$.** A global section of $\mathcal{I}_\Delta \otimes \mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}$ is a sum $\sigma = \sum_i s_i \otimes t_i$ (with $s_i, t_i \in \Gamma(X, \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n})$) vanishing on $\Delta$. Its image in the fiber at $(x,x)$ under (4.2) is computed as follows. Write $s_i = s_i(x) + s_i'$ and $t_i = t_i(x) + t_i'$ with $s_i', t_i' \in \mathfrak{m}_x \cdot \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}|_x$. The vanishing $\sum_i s_i(x)\,t_i(x) = 0$ (from $\sigma|_\Delta = 0$) kills the constant term, and the image in $\mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}_x^2 \otimes L_x \otimes L_x$ is

$$\operatorname{im}(\sigma) \;=\; \sum_i \bigl(s_i(x)\, t_i' + t_i(x)\, s_i'\bigr) \;\in\; \mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}_x^2 \otimes_k L_x \otimes_k L_x, \tag{4.3}$$

where $L_x := \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}|_x \cong k$.

Now we use the **"fix the second factor"** construction. Fix a nonzero element $\mu_0 \in L_x$ (i.e., $\mu_0 = 1$ under the trivialization $L_x \cong k$). Since (4.2) is surjective, for any $\alpha \in \mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}_x^2$ there exists a section $\sigma = \sum_i s_i \otimes t_i$ whose image (4.3) equals $\alpha \otimes \mu_0 \otimes \mu_0 \in \mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}_x^2 \otimes L_x \otimes L_x$.

Define

$$s \;=\; \sum_i t_i(x)\, s_i' \;\in\; \Gamma(X,\; \mathfrak{m}_x\,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}|_x) \;\subset\; \Gamma(X, \mathcal{I}_x \otimes \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}).$$

More precisely, $s$ is obtained from $\sigma$ by "contracting the second factor at $x$": we replace each $t_i$ by its value $t_i(x) \in L_x \cong k$ and each $s_i$ by its first-order part $s_i' = s_i - s_i(x)$. The resulting section $s$ lies in $\Gamma(X, \mathcal{I}_x \otimes \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n})$ because each $s_i'$ vanishes at $x$.

Its image in $(\mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}_x^2)\otimes_k L_x$ is

$$\operatorname{im}(s) \;=\; \sum_i t_i(x)\, s_i' \;\in\; \mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}_x^2 \otimes_k L_x.$$

We must check that this equals $\alpha \otimes \mu_0$. From (4.3) and the choice $\operatorname{im}(\sigma) = \alpha \otimes \mu_0 \otimes \mu_0$, expanding in the basis $\mu_0$ of the one-dimensional space $L_x$:

$$\alpha \otimes \mu_0 \otimes \mu_0 \;=\; \sum_i \bigl(s_i(x)\, t_i' + t_i(x)\, s_i'\bigr).$$

The two summands live in $L_x \otimes (\mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}_x^2 \otimes L_x)$ and $(\mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}_x^2 \otimes L_x) \otimes L_x$ respectively. By fixing the *second* factor at $\mu_0$ (i.e., projecting onto the second summand by evaluating the first factor's $L_x$-component), we extract exactly $\sum_i t_i(x)\, s_i' = \alpha \otimes \mu_0$.

Concretely: since $L_x \cong k$ is one-dimensional, we may identify $\mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}_x^2 \otimes L_x \otimes L_x \cong \mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}_x^2$. Under this identification, the image (4.3) is $\sum_i (s_i(x)\, t_i' + t_i(x)\, s_i')$, and the "fix the second factor at $\mu_0=1$" projection picks out the $t_i(x)\, s_i'$ part, which equals $\alpha$ by construction. Hence

$$\operatorname{im}(s) \;=\; \alpha \otimes \mu_0 \;\in\; \mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}_x^2 \otimes_k L_x.$$

Since $\alpha \in \mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}_x^2$ was arbitrary and $\mu_0$ is a basis of $L_x$, the map

$$\Gamma(X, \mathcal{I}_x \otimes \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}) \;\longrightarrow\; (\mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}_x^2)\otimes_k \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n}|_x$$

is surjective. This is condition (3), and it holds for every closed point $x$ and every $n \geq n_2$.

---

### Conclusion

Set $N = \max(n_1, n_2)$. For every $n \geq N$:

- Condition (1) holds by Step 1 (since $n \geq n_1$).
- Condition (2) holds by Step 3 (since $n \geq n_2$).
- Condition (3) holds by Step 4 (since $n \geq n_2$).

Therefore $\varphi_n : X \to \mathbb{P}(\Gamma(X, \mathcal{L}^{\otimes n})^\vee)$ is an immersion. Since $X$ is proper over $k$, the image of $\varphi_n$ is closed in the projective target, so $\varphi_n$ is a **closed immersion**.

$$\boxed{\text{Yes. There exists } n \geq 1 \text{ such that } \varphi_n : X \to \mathbb{P}(\Gamma(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes n})^\vee) \text{ is a closed immersion.}}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
