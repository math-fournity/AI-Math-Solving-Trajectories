Let \( F \in \mathbb{C}[X,Y] \) be an irreducible polynomial and \( n \in \mathbb{N} \), \( n \ge 1 \), with \( p_i \in \mathbb{C}[X] \) for \( 0 \le i \le n \), such that \[ F(X,Y) = \sum\limits_{i=0}^{n} p_i(X) Y^{n-i}. \] Suppose \( x \in \mathbb{C} \) is such that \( p_0(x) \neq 0 \), so \( F(x,Y) \) has \( n \) zeros, and one of these \( n \) zeros is a single one (i.e., there exists \( y \in \mathbb{C} \) such that \( F(x,y) = 0 \) and \( \frac{\partial F}{\partial Y}(x,y) \neq 0 \)). Is it possible for the polynomial \( F(x,Y) \) to have a multiple zero?
