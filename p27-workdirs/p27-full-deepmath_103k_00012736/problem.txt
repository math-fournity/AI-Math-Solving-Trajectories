Consider a continuous function $f: \mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^n$ with the property that there exists $c \in (0,1)$ such that for all $x,y \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\left\| f(x) - f(y) \right\| \leq c \left\| x-y \right\|$. From Banach's fixed-point theorem, for any $x^{(0)} = x_0 \in \mathbb{R}^n$, the sequence $x^{(k+1)} := f(x^{(k)})$ converges to the unique fixed-point $\bar{x}$ of $f$. Now consider the sequence $y^{(0)} = x_0$ and $y^{(k)} := f\left( \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} y^{(i)} \right)$. Determine the limit $\lim_{k \rightarrow \infty} y^{(k)}$. Does it converge to the fixed point $\bar{x}$?
