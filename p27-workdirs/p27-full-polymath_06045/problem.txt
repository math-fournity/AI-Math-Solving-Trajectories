In the competitive world of high-efficiency urban farming, a "Balanced Harvest" is defined as a crop yield of $N$ units that can be stored in a rectangular shipping container with a base length of $a$ meters and a base width of $b$ meters. For a container to be considered "Balanced," the dimensions must be positive integers such that the length $a$ is no greater than the width $b$, but the width $b$ must not exceed the length $a$ by more than one-third (mathematically, $a \leq b \leq \frac{4}{3}a$).
