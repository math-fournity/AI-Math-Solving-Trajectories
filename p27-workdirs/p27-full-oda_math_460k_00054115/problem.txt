Let the function \( f(t) \) be continuously differentiable and positive for \( 0 < t < 1 \), and also let \( \int_0^1 f(t) \, dt \) exist. The entire function defined by the integral\n\n\[ \int_0^1 f(t) e^{xt} \, dt = F(z) \]\n\nhas no zeros\n\n- in the half-plane \(\Re z \geq 0\), if \( f'(t) > 0 \),\n- in the half-plane \(\Re z \leq 0\), if \( f'(t) < 0 \).\n\n[Not analogously to III 205 through a limiting process from III 22, but by appropriate translation of the proof of III 22.]
