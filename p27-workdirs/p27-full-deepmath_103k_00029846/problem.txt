Let $f:[0,1]\times [0,1] \to \mathbf{R}$ be an infinitely differentiable, non-negative function with finitely many zeros. Suppose $(a,b)\in [0,1]\times [0,1]$ is a zero of $f$. The Taylor series of $f$ centered at $(a,b)$ is given by $$ \widetilde{f}(x,\xi) = \sum_{0\leq i+j< \infty}\frac{\partial^{i+j}f(a,b)}{\partial x^i \partial \xi^j}\frac{(x-a)^i(\xi-b)^j}{i!j!}, $$ which converges for points in the ball $B_{\delta}$ with radius $\delta>0$, and for such points, $f(x,\xi) = \widetilde{f}(x,\xi)$. Assume the first nonzero derivative of $f$ is $\frac{\partial^2}{\partial \xi^2}f(a,b)$, and there exists $n\geq 1$ such that $\frac{\partial^n}{\partial x^n}f(a,b) \ne 0$. Determine if there exist positive constants $c,k, \widetilde{\delta}$ such that for $(x,\xi)\in B_{\widetilde{\delta}}$, $$ |f(x,y)|\geq c ((x-a)^2+(\xi-b)^2)^{\frac{k}{2}}. $$
