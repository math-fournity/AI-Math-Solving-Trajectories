$A, B, C, D, E$ and $F$ are members of a six-member group. The group members share $n$ pieces of chocolate in such a way that each of them gets at least one piece, and $A$ gets fewer than $B$, who gets fewer than $C$, who gets fewer than $D$, who gets fewer than $E$, and finally $F$ gets the most. The group members know these conditions, the value of $n$, and of course how many pieces of chocolate they themselves receive, but they have no other information. What is the smallest value of $n$ for which the distribution can be carried out in such a way that none of them can accurately state how many pieces of chocolate each of the others received?
