Consider a circulant $n \times n$ matrix $C$ over a finite field $F$ of order $q$, with the first row $c_0, c_1, \ldots, c_{n-1} \in F$. The determinant of $C$ can be expressed in terms of its representer polynomial $p_C(x) = c_0 + c_1 x + \cdots + c_{n-1}x^{n-1}$ and the roots of unity. A matrix $C$ is invertible if and only if $0$ is not an eigenvalue. Given that there are $q^n$ circulant matrices over $F$, determine how many of these matrices are invertible. Specifically, consider the case where $F=\mathbb{Z}_p$ and $C$ is a $(p-1) \times (p-1)$ matrix.
