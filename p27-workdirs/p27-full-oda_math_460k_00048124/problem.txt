28. Let $n$ be a positive integer. A sequence of $n$ positive integers (which may be equal) $a_{1}, a_{2}, \cdots, a_{n}$ is called a "full sequence" if it satisfies the following condition: for any positive integer $k (k \geqslant 2)$, if $k$ is one of the terms in the sequence, then $k-1$ is also a term in the sequence, and the first occurrence of $k-1$ in the sequence is before the last occurrence of $k$. How many "full sequences" are there for each $n$?
