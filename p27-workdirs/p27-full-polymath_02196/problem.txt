Initially, a three-digit natural number \( n \) is written on the board. Two players, A and B, take turns, with A going first. The player whose turn it is reduces the number on the board by one of its own divisors (i.e., different from \( 1 \) and the number itself). For example, if at some point the number on the board is \( 6 \), it can be reduced by \( 2 \), after which the number on the board will be \( 4 \). The player who cannot make a move loses, and the other wins. It is known that player A has a way to win, as does player B. How many possible \( n \) are there?
