Let \(ABC\) be a triangle and let \(P\) be a point inside the triangle. Let \(E, F, G\) be the second intersections of \(AP\), \(BP\), \(CP\) with the circumcircle of the triangle, respectively. Let \(H\) be the intersection of \(EF\) and \(AC\), and \(I\) the intersection of \(EG\) and \(AB\). Let \(P_a\) be the intersection of \(BH\) and \(CI\). We define \(P_b\) and \(P_c\) similarly. If \(AP_a\), \(BP_b\), and \(CP_c\) intersect at a common point \(Q\), calculate the ratio \(\frac{AQ}{QP_a}\) when \(P\) is the centroid of \(\triangle ABC\).
