Let $X$ be a set of 10,000 integers, none of which is divisible by 47. We wish to find a subset $Y \subseteq X$ such that for any elements $a, b, c, d, e \in Y$ (not necessarily distinct), the sum $a - b + c - d + e$ is not divisible by 47. Using the Erdős-style probabilistic method or pigeonhole principle over the residues modulo 47, what is the largest integer $k$ such that we can always guarantee the existence of such a subset $Y$ with at least $k$ elements?
