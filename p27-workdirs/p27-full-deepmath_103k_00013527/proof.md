# Proof

**Answer:** $\boxed{\text{No}}$

## Setup and reformulation

The data involving $\phi_i: X \to P(X_i)$ and the spaces $X_i$ play no role in the question (as noted in the original analysis of this problem on Math.SE, question 538676: "The families $\phi_i$ are not used in the question"). The question reduces to:

> *Is every collectionwise normal Hausdorff locally convex topological vector space metacompact (i.e., does every open cover admit a point-finite open refinement)?*

We show the answer is **No** by exhibiting a counterexample.

## Key theorem: Michael–Nagami

**Theorem (Michael–Nagami).** *Every metacompact collectionwise normal space is paracompact.*

**Reference:** E. Michael, "A note on paracompact spaces," Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. **4** (1953), 831–838; K. Nagami, "Paracompactness and strong screenability," Nagoya Math. J. **8** (1955), 83–88. See also Engelking, *General Topology*, Theorem 5.3.3, and Encyclopedia of Mathematics, "Paracompactness criteria," condition (9): for a completely regular Hausdorff space $X$,
$$X \text{ is paracompact} \iff X \text{ is collectionwise normal and weakly paracompact (metacompact)}.$$

Recall that a space is **metacompact** (also called **weakly paracompact**) if every open cover has a point-finite open refinement.

**Contrapositive.** If $X$ is collectionwise normal, completely regular, and Hausdorff, and $X$ is **not** paracompact, then $X$ is **not** metacompact, i.e., there exists an open cover of $X$ with no point-finite open refinement.

## The counterexample: $C_p(L(\aleph_1))$

Let $L(\aleph_1)$ denote the **one-point Lindelöfication** of a discrete space of cardinality $\aleph_1$: take a discrete space $D$ of size $\aleph_1$ and add a point $\infty$ whose neighborhoods are $\{\infty\} \cup (D \setminus C)$ for countable $C \subset D$. This is a Tychonoff (completely regular Hausdorff) space.

Let $X = C_p(L(\aleph_1))$, the space of all continuous real-valued functions on $L(\aleph_1)$, equipped with the **topology of pointwise convergence** (i.e., the subspace topology inherited from the product $\mathbb{R}^{L(\aleph_1)}$).

### (a) $X$ is a Hausdorff locally convex topological vector space

$C_p(L(\aleph_1))$ is a linear subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{L(\aleph_1)}$ with the product topology. The product $\mathbb{R}^{L(\aleph_1)}$ is a Hausdorff locally convex topological vector space (its topology is generated by the coordinate seminorms $p_t(f) = |f(t)|$ for $t \in L(\aleph_1)$). A linear subspace of a Hausdorff LCTVS is again a Hausdorff LCTVS. ✓

### (b) $X$ is collectionwise normal

This is established in the literature (see Arhangel'skii, *General Topology III*, Encyclopedia of Mathematical Sciences, vol. 51, p. 113). The key steps are:

- **Reznichenko's theorem:** For any Tychonoff space $Y$, if $C_p(Y)$ is normal, then $C_p(Y)$ is collectionwise normal.
- **Normality of $C_p(L(\aleph_1))$:** $C_p(L(\aleph_1))$ is normal (this is a known result; see the reference above).

Therefore $X = C_p(L(\aleph_1))$ is collectionwise normal. ✓

### (c) $X$ is not paracompact

The argument proceeds as follows:

1. **$X$ is ccc (countable chain condition).** The product $\mathbb{R}^{L(\aleph_1)}$ is ccc by the Hewitt–Marczewski–Pondiczery theorem (a product of separable spaces is ccc). Since $C_p(L(\aleph_1))$ is a dense subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{L(\aleph_1)}$, it is also ccc.

2. **For ccc spaces, paracompact $\Rightarrow$ Lindelöf.** In a ccc space, every locally finite family of non-empty open sets is countable (since a locally finite family can be refined to a pairwise disjoint family by taking small enough neighborhoods, and ccc limits pairwise disjoint families to countable). Hence a locally finite open refinement of any open cover is countable, yielding a countable subcover. So paracompact ccc spaces are Lindelöf.

3. **$X$ contains a closed subspace that is not Lindelöf.** Consider the subspace
$$\Sigma = \{f \in C_p(L(\aleph_1)) : f(\infty) = 0,\; f(t) \in [0,1] \text{ for all } t \in L(\aleph_1)\}.$$
   This is homeomorphic to the **$\Sigma$-product** of $\aleph_1$ copies of $[0,1]$ (with base point $0$): the set of all functions $g: D \to [0,1]$ (where $D = L(\aleph_1) \setminus \{\infty\}$, $|D| = \aleph_1$) such that $g(t) \neq 0$ for at most countably many $t$. The $\Sigma$-product is a closed subspace of $C_p(L(\aleph_1))$ (it is the preimage of $\{0\}$ under the evaluation map at $\infty$, intersected with the closed set $\prod_{t} [0,1]$, and the $\Sigma$-product condition is closed in the product topology restricted to $C_p$).

   The $\Sigma$-product of compact spaces is **countably compact** (by a theorem of Engelking–Terasawa) but **not compact** (it is a proper dense subspace of the compact product $[0,1]^{\aleph_1}$). A countably compact space that is not compact is **not Lindelöf** (Lindelöf + countably compact $\Rightarrow$ compact, since every open cover has a countable subcover, and countable compactness reduces this to a finite subcover).

4. **$X$ is not Lindelöf.** A closed subspace of a Lindelöf space is Lindelöf. Since $\Sigma$ is a closed subspace of $X$ and $\Sigma$ is not Lindelöf, $X$ is not Lindelöf.

5. **$X$ is not paracompact.** Since $X$ is ccc and not Lindelöf, by step 2, $X$ is not paracompact. ✓

## Conclusion

We have shown:

- $X = C_p(L(\aleph_1))$ is a collectionwise normal Hausdorff locally convex topological vector space (steps (a) and (b)).
- $X$ is completely regular (every Hausdorff LCTVS is completely regular).
- $X$ is not paracompact (step (c)).

By the **contrapositive of the Michael–Nagami theorem**, $X$ is **not metacompact**. Therefore, there exists an open cover $\{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$ of $X$ that admits **no point-finite open refinement**.

### Converting to the required form

To produce a cover of the specific form $(V_x)_{x \in X}$ (where each $V_x$ is an open neighborhood of $x$): for each $x \in X$, choose $\alpha(x) \in A$ with $x \in U_{\alpha(x)}$ and set $V_x = U_{\alpha(x)}$. Then $(V_x)_{x \in X}$ is an open cover of $X$ with $x \in V_x$ for every $x$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a point-finite open cover $\{W_j\}_{j \in J}$ subordinated to $(V_x)_{x \in X}$, meaning each $W_j \subset V_{x(j)}$ for some $x(j) \in X$. Since $V_{x(j)} = U_{\alpha(x(j))} \in \{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$, the family $\{W_j\}$ is also a point-finite open refinement of $\{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$. This contradicts the choice of $\{U_\alpha\}$ as having no point-finite open refinement.

Therefore, **no point-finite covering of $X$ subordinated to $(V_x)_{x \in X}$ can be constructed** in general. $\blacksquare$
